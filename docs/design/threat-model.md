# LookFirst threat model

Status: proposed (LF-1). Method: STRIDE over the data flow in `architecture.md`.

## Assets and attackers

Assets: the user's device and accounts (credentials, OTP seeds, money), the user's IP and location, the integrity of the verdict (the user's trust), the local history.

Attackers:
- **A1, printer:** sticker on a parking meter, flyer, poster, or a QR in a phishing e-mail or PDF. Controls the QR content and the destination site. Cheap, mass scale. Main adversary.
- **A2, targeted:** crafts a payload against the analyser itself (evasion, crash, confusing the verdict screen).
- **A3, network:** on-path attacker (public Wi-Fi) when lists are downloaded.
- **A4, supply chain:** compromises a list publisher or its hosting, or a dependency or CI step of the app.
- **A5, other app on the device:** intercepts Intents or tries to read app storage.

Out of scope: rooted or compromised device, malicious OS, physical coercion. A legitimate QR physically overlaid by a fake one is A1: the app can only judge content.

Trust boundaries: (1) QR pixels -> decoder; (2) decoded string -> parser; (3) app -> system (Intents, clipboard, Wi-Fi); (4) app -> network; (5) app -> local storage.

Status legend: **M** mitigated by design; **A** accepted explicitly (reason given). Letters in the text refer to STRIDE: Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege.

## QR content (boundaries 1 and 2)

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-CNT-1 | Huge payload (QR holds up to ~3 KB) exhausting memory or UI (D) | Hard cap on raw length (4096 chars, above = rejected with a message); UI truncates the middle, full text on demand; analysers have bounded cost (edit distance only on hostname labels, host max 253 chars) | M |
| T-CNT-2 | Control characters (NUL, CR/LF, ESC, U+2028) for UI/log injection or field splitting (T, S) | Normalise NFC; C0/C1 and format characters are escaped visibly and count as a signal; raw content never logged | M |
| T-CNT-3 | Bidi override/isolate (U+202E, U+2066-2069) to display a spoofed or reversed URL (S) | Detected and shown as `<U+202E>`; inside a host or URL it raises `DANGEROUS`; decoded content rendered in an isolated LTR container | M |
| T-CNT-4 | Homoglyph hosts (Cyrillic `а` for Latin `a`), mixed scripts, fullwidth forms (S) | Convert host with an embedded UTS #46 implementation (ICU4J or equivalent; `java.net.IDN` is IDNA2003 and `android.icu.text.IDNA` needs API 24+, minSdk is 23) to ASCII and Unicode forms; mixed-script and confusable check (UTS #39 subset); always display both forms | M (heuristic, see T-CNT-6) |
| T-CNT-5 | Punycode `xn--` hosts hiding the real name (S) | Decode, show decoded form with a reason; invalid punycode = `SUSPICIOUS` | M |
| T-CNT-6 | Look-alikes and brand-new malicious domains that no list knows (S) | Edit-distance and keyword heuristics vs the shipped brand list. New domains can slip through | A: a local-only tool cannot know unreported domains; the UI says "no risk signal detected", never "safe" |
| T-CNT-7 | Parsing differentials: `https://paypal.com@evil.tld`, backslashes, `//`, userinfo, decimal/octal/hex IPs, trailing dots, odd ports (S) | One strict parser (RFC 3986 plus WHATWG-style normalisation) for analysis and for opening; the normalised URL that was analysed is the one opened, passed to the browser in canonical form (punycode host, percent-encoded URL) and `Uri.getHost()` is re-checked equal to the analysed host just before the Intent; userinfo and numeric hosts are signals. Rule "analysed = opened": same effective scheme (`effectiveWebScheme`, a scheme-less `host:port/path` is https) for the canonical URL, the reputation lookup and the Open action (offered only if the canonical URL exists); host looked up = host of the canonical URL that is opened (`openedHost`, IPv4 in dotted decimal); exception: IPv6 literals, which the lists do not index (LF-30) | M |
| T-CNT-8 | Shorteners hide the destination (S) | Embedded shortener list, flagged `CAUTION`; optional redirect tracing | M (resolution is optional by D9) |
| T-CNT-8b | Link to a local service or a box (CSRF against `localhost`, `router`, `fritz.box`, `*.local`, `*.home.arpa`) or to a name that exists nowhere (S) | `LOCAL_HOST` (`SUSPICIOUS`): single label, `localhost`, reserved (RFC 6761 / 6762 / 8375) and private-use suffixes (`.internal`, `.lan`...), known box names (`fritz.box`; `.box` is a real gTLD), TLD absent from the ICANN part of the embedded PSL; numeric hosts keep `IP_HOST`; the tracer already refuses local targets, but only after resolution (a DNS query leaves); tracing is not short-circuited (LF-34) | M |
| T-CNT-9 | Payloads built to crash or stall the analyser: nested encodings, giant labels, regex DoS (D) | No backtracking-prone regex on untrusted input; analysis on a background dispatcher with a timeout; timeout = `CAUTION` "could not fully analyse" | M |
| T-CNT-10 | Plain-text QR with social engineering ("call this number", fake support) (S) | Text with phone numbers, scheme-less URLs or crypto addresses gets `CAUTION`; text is never auto-linkified. Note: a `Text` such as `domain.example/x` (not openable in the app, but copyable) gets only `CAUTION`, with no list lookup today (follow-up LF-42) | M |

## Dangerous schemes and opening (boundary 3)

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-SCH-1 | `intent://...#Intent;component=...;end` launching arbitrary activities or injecting extras (E) | Never call `Intent.parseUri` on scan data. `intent:` is `DANGEROUS`, Copy only | M |
| T-SCH-2 | `javascript:`, `data:`, `vbscript:` (E) | `DANGEROUS`, not openable | M |
| T-SCH-3 | `file:`, `content:`, `android-app:` (local file or provider access) (I, E) | Blocked, Copy only | M |
| T-SCH-4 | App deep links (`myapp://pay?...`, `market://`, wallet or messaging apps) triggering in-app actions, confused deputy (E) | Allow-list of openable schemes: `http`, `https`, `tel`, `sms`/`smsto`, `mailto`, `geo`, `WIFI` (internal). `otpauth` is Copy only (T-OTP). Any other scheme (deep links, D12): `CAUTION`, scheme shown, **Copy only**, no Open button and no setting to enable one | M |
| T-SCH-5 | `tel:` with premium-rate numbers or USSD codes (`*#06#`, `*21*...#`) (E) | `ACTION_DIAL` only, never `ACTION_CALL`; `*`/`#` sequences and short codes flagged `SUSPICIOUS`; premium-rate detection is best effort | M (premium detection partial) |
| T-SCH-6 | `sms:` with prefilled body subscribing to paid services or leaking data (E) | `ACTION_SENDTO` with `smsto:`, user must press send, body shown in the verdict, short codes flagged | M |
| T-SCH-7 | `mailto:` with bcc, attach or body tricks (I) | Parse into fields, show recipient and subject, ignore attachments, `ACTION_SENDTO` without attachment extras | M |
| T-SCH-8 | Action triggered without confirmation (E) | ADR-0003: only `ActionExecutor` fires Intents, called solely from a button handler; an architecture test (`IntentLaunchRulesTest`, regex over the sources of `app/src/main`) forbids `startActivity`, `Intent(`, `parseUri`, `PendingIntent`, URI handlers, `Linkify`, broadcasts and services outside an explicit allow-list (`data/platform`, plus the result launchers of `ScanRoute` and `ResultRoute`); no auto-open setting exists | M |
| T-SCH-9 | Badly built or interceptable Intent: implicit Intent, wrong category, another app claiming the scheme (A5) | http(s): `ACTION_VIEW` + `CATEGORY_BROWSABLE` with a scheme-checked Uri; no chooser: tel, sms, mailto and geo go to the user's default app (the user chose it), and the SMS Intent is sent with `setPackage(Telephony.Sms.getDefaultSmsPackage())`; no secret (Wi-Fi password, OTP seed) in any implicit Intent; no exported components except the launcher activity; no `PendingIntent` | M; a malicious app that the user set as default (browser, dialer, SMS, mail, maps) is an accepted risk (A): the OS and the user choose the default |
| T-SCH-12 | A verified App Link or a registered handler captures an `https://` `ACTION_VIEW` so a non-browser app opens the URL (E, A5) | Resolve the default browser and `setPackage` on it; if a non-browser handler exists for the URL, show it in the verdict (best effort: an App Link that declares a host may be invisible with the host-less `<queries>`, and `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` is not requested) | M, partly: the opening is still forced into the default browser, only the warning can be missing; a malicious default browser is an accepted risk (A) |
| T-SCH-10 | A WebView inside LookFirst would run attacker content in our process (E) | No WebView. External default browser only (no Custom Tabs at MVP: no `androidx.browser` dependency), so the browser's own protections apply. `<queries>` declares only the browser and App Link probes; `warmup()`, `mayLaunchUrl()` and any prefetch with scanned data are forbidden (they would contact the site before the user decides) | M |
| T-SCH-11 | Clipboard exposure of copied secrets (I) | Copy is an explicit tap; for secrets (otpauth, Wi-Fi password): confirmation first, `ClipDescription.EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE` (API 33+, hides the preview), and `clearPrimaryClip()` (API 28+; API 23-27: replaced by an empty clip) when the user returns to LookFirst, also after a process death (the random clip label, never the secret, is persisted in a private backup-excluded file); the flag is set from API 24 and honoured at the keyboard's discretion | M; since API 29 a background app cannot read the clipboard, but the foreground app and keyboard/IME can (A, OS limitation) |

## Type-specific payloads

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-OTP | `otpauth://` QR that enrols an attacker-controlled TOTP, or a lure "re-enrol your 2FA"; seed leaking to clipboard or history (S, I) | Parse issuer, account, algorithm, digits, period; warn that a legitimate service shows a setup QR only while the user configures 2FA in their own session; flag issuer/label mismatch and unusual parameters; secret masked in UI, never stored, never logged; Copy only at MVP, with confirmation and the sensitive flag; never an implicit Intent (the URI contains the seed) | M; social-engineering residual A (the app can only warn) |
| T-WIFI | Evil twin or open network in `WIFI:S:..;T:..;P:..;;`, hidden SSID, `nopass`, WEP, EAP configs (S, I) | Parse the standard format including `\` escapes; open/WEP networks `CAUTION`; SSID shown with control chars made visible; API 30+: `ACTION_WIFI_ADD_NETWORKS` (system confirmation, Intent restricted to system packages); below 30: credentials displayed and the Wi-Fi settings opened, nothing configured silently; no Wi-Fi suggestions; EAP data shown, not auto-applied | M |
| T-PAY | Modified payment QR: EPC/SEPA (IBAN, BIC, name, amount, reference), `bitcoin:`/`ethereum:` (T, S) | Show IBAN (grouped), beneficiary name, amount, currency and reference prominently; validate IBAN checksum and EPC structure; flag missing name, invalid crypto address checksum (Base58Check, Bech32, EIP-55), `ethereum:` with contract-call data; always "check recipient and amount with the issuer" | A for a valid but attacker-owned destination: unknowable locally |
| T-APP | QR login or device-pairing lures (QRLJacking style) (S) | URL patterns for pairing/login flows get a specific `CAUTION` reason | M (heuristic) |

## Reputation lists (boundary 4)

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-LST-1 | MITM on the download (A3) | HTTPS only, system TLS validation, no cleartext fallback (Network Security Config), redirects only to the same host over https; a bad certificate ends the attempt | M |
| T-LST-2 | Compromised publisher, CDN or mirror serving a forged list (A4) | No signature of ours (D20, ADR-0008): a list can only add warnings, never lower a verdict, never change the PSL, brands or RDAP server; the embedded official-domain list is consulted first; size cap, entry bounds, 2 % invalid-line limit, variation limit against the installed index on the set difference, not the count (entries removed and entries added, each at most 10 % + 2 points per day of age, none after 45 days; LF-32); a refusal keeps the previous index | A: within the variation limit a compromised publisher can add false warnings on non-official domains or drop real entries slowly (10 % at most when the list is fresh, more as the phone ages). The `Date` header is untrusted publisher data and can only harden (stale warning, lower recorded time bounded by the clock), never relax a limit. No key to steal |
| T-LST-8 | Rollback or replay of an old list (T) | Not applicable as such: lists are replaced by the current publisher file; an old file is only a staler list. Age is measured from the last successful check against `max(clock, latest time ever seen)` and shown, older than 14 days adds a `CAUTION` reason; a clock set back cannot make a list younger; a clock set far ahead at one download is not corrected by `Date` (untrusted); it shows as unknown age, `LISTS_STALE` (CAUTION), with the limit kept (LF-32); residual: a frozen age can keep updates refused, a denial of service the publisher can already cause | M |
| T-LST-3 | Oversized or malformed download, decompression bomb, hostile lines (D) | Streamed byte cap per source (8 MiB, counted while reading, `Content-Length` never trusted), no archive or compression layer, parser in constant memory per line (300-byte line limit, 64 KiB chunks, no regex), entry cap, download to a temporary file, index written and validated before an atomic rename; previous index kept on any failure | M |
| T-LST-4 | Poisoned list: popular domains inserted so the app cries wolf, or real entries removed (T) | Embedded official-domain list consulted first: no list entry ever applies to an official registrable domain or to any host under it (`accounts.google.com` included), and such entries are counted and not indexed; a list with more than 100 of them is refused; entry minimum (40 000 for CERT Polska, revise if the list grows) and maximum; variation limit; a list never lowers a verdict. Exception (LF-31): domains where anyone gets a sub-domain and that are not in the private PSL (`UserContentPlatforms`: wordpress.com, free.fr, ovh.net, slack.com) are official only for the host itself, `www.` and an embedded list of reserved service labels per platform (`mobile.free.fr`, `api.slack.com`, `public-api.wordpress.com`...); `name.platform` is a registrable name, indexed, looked up and judged by the brand matcher like `name.blogspot.com`; `vps.ovh.net` is a sub-suffix (tenant `vps-xxx.vps.ovh.net`). Residual: phishing hosted under a service subdomain of another official domain is not flagged by lists; on a platform, a service label missing from the reserved list is handled as a tenant (possible false positive, and a poisoned entry on it would be indexed and shown, under the volume limits), and a platform not in the list is a gap (reviewed with each release; `wetransfer.com` is left out: paid plans only, service labels not enumerable) | M for official domains; A for other domains (see T-LST-2) |
| T-LST-5 | Stale lists give false reassurance (R) | List age shown in every verdict; older than 14 days adds a `CAUTION` reason; no list at all gives "lists missing" until the first download (no seed in the APK, D20), which starts at the end of the first-launch screen | M |
| T-LST-6 | The update request reveals the user's IP to the list publisher, or lets it tag the phone (I) | No identifier, no cookie, no scan data, generic UA. `If-Modified-Since` is a date the app formats itself from the publisher's `Last-Modified`, parsed strictly (IMF-fixdate, not in the future) and stored as an instant: the server cannot choose bytes that come back. Unconditional download every 7 days at most apart. The publisher is named in About | A: the IP is inherent to any download; documented in the privacy notice |
| T-LST-7 | Licence breach (legal) | We redistribute nothing: the phone downloads the file from the publisher and builds a private index. Terms of each source are quoted in `reputation-sources.md`; CERT Polska allows use "for any purpose, including commercial purposes" | To confirm in writing for automated use by an app (open point) |

## Optional network actions (boundary 4)

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-NET-1 | Redirect tracing tells the attacker that the QR was scanned, from which IP; the server can fingerprint bots and serve a benign page (I) | Optional per-use button, warning before use; no cookies, no body, generic UA, 5 hops and 5 s | A: inherent to the feature, consent explicit (D9). Note that a benign answer to the tracer proves nothing |
| T-NET-2 | Redirect to non-http schemes or to the user's LAN (router admin, SSRF-like, DNS rebinding), or huge responses (E, D) | The tracer resolves the host itself (once per trace), checks EVERY returned address and refuses the host if one is blocked, then connects a socket to a checked IP (no OkHttp: `HttpURLConnection`/OkHttp cannot be told which IP to use without a custom `Dns`, and a hand-written HEAD adds no dependency); TLS with SNI = host, certificate checked against the host by subject alternative names only; response head capped at 16 KiB, body never read; a `Location` goes through the same parser and host normalizer as a scan; blocked ranges: IPv4 0/8, 10/8, 100.64/10, 127/8, 169.254/16, 172.16/12, 192.0.0/24, 192.0.2/24, 192.168/16, 198.18/15, 198.51.100/24, 203.0.113/24, 224/4 and 240/4 (multicast, reserved, broadcast); IPv6 ::/96 (unspecified, loopback, IPv4-compatible), fc00::/7, fe80::/10, fec0::/10, ff00::/8, 100::/64, 2001::/32 (Teredo), 2001:db8::/32, 64:ff9b:1::/48; IPv4-mapped (`::ffff:a.b.c.d`), NAT64 (64:ff9b::/96) and 6to4 (2002::/16) are judged as their embedded IPv4; hops followed by hand (max 5), `Proxy.NO_PROXY`; https only and port 443 only (a link or hop on another port is never contacted: it cannot be used to probe services on public hosts); HEAD or `Range` request; body never read; certificate wildcards over a public suffix refused; at most two worker threads at a time, shared by both optional actions: two DNS lookups that never return can occupy both for a while, and a new request is then refused with "try again in a moment" (nothing is sent) until they are released. Same rules for RDAP. Follow-up for the phone milestone: instrumented test of SNI and hostname checking on API 23 against a local `SSLServerSocket` | M |
| T-NET-3 | RDAP exposes the domain to the registry; untrusted JSON response, hostile bootstrap data or redirect (I, T, E) | Only the registrable domain of the ICANN section of the embedded Public Suffix List is sent (never a path, a subdomain or the full address), to the `https` server the IANA bootstrap embedded in the APK names for its TLD (pinned by SHA-256 in the generator, the committed copy is checked against the pin by the data-provenance workflow, which also warns (without failing) when IANA publishes a new file; reviewed by hand and renewed with each app version, see reputation-sources.md: there is no downloaded bundle that could be compromised to redirect RDAP). Only URLs that are `https`, with a host name rather than an address, no port, no userinfo, no query and a path ending in `/` are used, and the build tests the real file. Same client as T-NET-2 (`BoundedWork`, `CheckedConnector`, `TlsHopTransport`: one DNS resolution, every address checked, connection to the checked IP on port 443, SNI and certificate checked for the host, 5 s for everything, no proxy, no cookie, constant User-Agent). A redirect is followed only to the same host over https (at most 3), anything else fails. Body read as a stream and capped at 256 KiB (`Content-Length`, `chunked` or end of connection; content coding, double framing and contradictory lengths refused); strict bounded JSON (depth 24, 20 000 values); only `objectClassName`, `ldhName` and the `registration` events are read, the name must be the one asked for, the date must be plausible (1985 to now + 1 day). Failure, 404 or an old date never lowers a verdict; 404 is information only | M; leak of the domain to the registry is A, disclosed in the dialog (which names the registry) |
| T-NET-4 | Following `http://` hops would need cleartext, and the Network Security Config is global (T) | No cleartext exception anywhere. A hop to `http://` is not followed and is shown as a risk signal | M |
| T-NET-5 | Domain age is an unreliable signal (new is not malicious, old can be compromised) (S) | Weak signal only, never lowers a verdict: under 30 days SUSPICIOUS, 30 to 89 days CAUTION, older says nothing (and the screen never says "safe") | M |

## Image scanning (boundary 1)

| ID | Threat | Counter-measure | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-IMG-1 | Booby-trapped image exploiting a native image decoder (E) | Platform decoders only (patched by OS updates), on a background thread; allowed types jpeg, png, webp, heif; no SVG or PDF | M; platform zero-day residual A |
| T-IMG-2 | Decompression bomb or CPU bomb: small file, huge dimensions, or a page of finder patterns (D) | Magic bytes checked before the decoder (jpeg, png, webp, heif); read at most 10 MiB (cancellable, descriptor closed); refuse above 25 MP declared; downsample to ~4 MP; catch `OutOfMemoryError`; ZXing's readers cannot be cancelled and are super-linear in the finder patterns (multi-code: 64 candidates 31 ms, 196 = 0.6 s, 289 = 2.4 s; plain TRY_HARDER: 289 = 13 ms, 729 = 82 ms), so `FinderGridGuard` counts candidates first, on the import and the camera path: multi-code reader only up to 64, plain reader only up to 400 (import fallback and camera), above that nothing is read (import: unreadable; camera: frame ignored). The count has a budget of 20 000 candidate checks (a page of 40 codes needs ~1 700, 12 codes ~1 000), since a check can walk a whole column (quadratic on tall stripes: 100x40000 took 18 s unbounded); over budget = grid. The importer also bounds the longest side to 2048 px, which bounds a column. Residual risk: a frame just under the thresholds costs at most a few hundred ms on the multi-code reader (64 candidates 31 ms on a PC, a phone is several times slower); one read at a time on a dedicated thread | M |
| T-IMG-3 | Image with several QRs so the user acts on the wrong one (S) | Only the first code in reading order is used and the Result screen says "several QR codes: only the first is shown" (D14: a choice among codes exposes the user to a decoy) | M |
| T-IMG-4 | Metadata leakage (EXIF) (I) | Image read once in memory, never copied, saved or uploaded | M |

## Residual risk (accepted)

Unknown and new malicious sites; valid payment destinations owned by the attacker; ignored warnings; platform zero-days; other apps registered as handlers; clipboard visible to the foreground app and keyboard; a compromised list publisher inside the variation limit (T-LST-2); the attacker learning of the scan when the user chooses redirect tracing.
